How I think about it

Great design isn't frictionless. It's calibrated.

Friction lives at the exact seam where what a person feels meets what the system needs. Verifying your identity with a bank should feel a little deliberate. Getting to your boarding pass shouldn't. My job is to know the difference, and my engineering years are why I can actually build for it.

About

I started as an engineer. I think that's my whole advantage.

How I got here

I spent my first chapter as a software engineer, close enough to the build to earn 24 patents. Then I moved into product design, left Capital One, and came back, which means I've seen the same systems from both the engineering side and the design side. That double vision is the thing I lead with.

How I work

My whole point of view is calibrating friction. Friction isn't the enemy, misplaced friction is. I'm good at telling the two apart because I know what it costs to build the thing on the other side of the screen. I use AI tools throughout my process, but the judgment is mine, and that's the part that matters.

A little more of me

Away from the screen I make things with my hands, ceramics, crochet, whatever the current hobby is. It's the same instinct as the day job: care about how a thing is made and how it feels to use. There's more of that over on Off the Clock.

What I'm looking for

A senior product design role on a mission-driven team. I'm in the DC area and open to remote or relocating for the right one.
